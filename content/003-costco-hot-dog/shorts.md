## Short 1 — The card is the product
- **Publish:** same day as the long video (Tue 8 Dec)
- **Hook text (frame 1):** Costco's real product isn't food
- **Narration (≈ 95 words):** Costco made about ten point four billion dollars in operating profit in fiscal twenty twenty-five. About half of it — five point three billion — came from one thing that never shows up at the checkout. The membership card. Membership fees were less than two percent of Costco's revenue, and about half of its operating profit. And ninety-two percent of members in the US and Canada renew. The hot dogs, the chicken, the cheap gas — they're the reason you keep paying for the card. Full receipt on Pricewhy.

## Short 2 — "Figure it out"
- **Publish:** Thu 10 Dec
- **Hook text:** "I will kill you." — Costco's founder
- **Narration (≈ 100 words):** Costco's former CEO Craig Jelinek once told his boss, co-founder Jim Sinegal: we can't sell this hot dog for a buck fifty — we're losing our rear ends. Sinegal's answer, as Jelinek tells it: "If you raise the effing hot dog, I will kill you. Figure it out." So Costco figured it out. It started making its own hot dogs, in its own plants, in Los Angeles and Chicago. And Jelinek says that today, at a dollar fifty, they make enough money to get a fair return. Not a loss leader. A promise. Full story on Pricewhy.

## Short 4 — 3 cents of every dollar
- **Publish:** Mon 14 Dec
- **Hook text:** Costco keeps $3 of your $100
- **Narration (≈ 90 words):** Here's where a hundred dollars at a Costco register went in fiscal twenty twenty-five. Eighty-eight eighty-eight paid for the goods. Nine twenty-five ran the business — wages, warehouses, trucks. That leaves one eighty-seven from selling you things. Membership fees add about another two dollars. After taxes, Costco keeps about three dollars. Three percent. That's the whole strategy: make as little as possible on what you buy, so you keep renewing the card. Full receipt on Pricewhy.

## Short 5 — Is Executive worth it? The math
- **Publish:** Wed 16 Dec
- **Hook text:** Executive membership: the $3,250 line
- **Narration (≈ 70 words):** Costco Executive costs sixty-five dollars more than Gold Star, and pays a two percent reward on qualifying purchases. So here's the only number you need: sixty-five divided by two percent is three thousand two hundred fifty. If you spend less than that on qualifying purchases in a year, the reward doesn't cover the upgrade. That's math, not advice. Full Costco breakdown on Pricewhy.
