## Short 1 — The theater's math (why popcorn, not the ticket)
- **Publish:** same day as the long video (Tue 1 Dec)
- **Hook text (frame 1):** Why popcorn, and not the ticket?
- **Narration (≈ 105 words):** Why does movie theater popcorn cost so much? Do the math from the theater's side. In twenty twenty-five, AMC kept about forty-nine cents of every ticket dollar in the US. The rest went to film costs, mostly the studio. Cinemark kept about forty-two cents. So if a theater adds a dollar to your ticket, the studio takes its cut of that dollar too. But add a dollar to the popcorn, and the corn doesn't cost a cent more. The theater keeps nearly all of it. That's why the snack bar, not the ticket, is where theaters make their money. Full breakdown on Pricewhy.

## Short 3 — Studios don't take 90%
- **Publish:** Sat 5 Dec
- **Hook text:** Studios don't take 90%
- **Narration (≈ 85 words):** You've probably heard that movie studios take ninety percent of your ticket. The annual reports say otherwise. In twenty twenty-five, AMC's film costs in the US were fifty-one point two percent of its ticket sales. At Cinemark, film rent and advertising together were fifty-eight percent. So: roughly half. On the biggest blockbusters, the share goes up. In twenty seventeen, the Wall Street Journal reported Disney's terms for The Last Jedi at about sixty-five percent. Big, but nowhere near ninety. Full breakdown on Pricewhy.

## Short 4 — AMC's problem isn't the popcorn
- **Publish:** Mon 7 Dec
- **Hook text:** AMC's problem isn't the popcorn
- **Narration (≈ 100 words):** AMC lost six hundred thirty-two point four million dollars in twenty twenty-five. Blame the popcorn? Follow the dollar. Per US visitor, after the studio, the snacks, the staff and the rent, AMC's US business made an operating profit of about thirty-four cents. Then came the debt: about two ninety-seven per visitor in interest. Company-wide, AMC's interest bill was five hundred thirty point two million. The popcorn isn't the problem. The debt is. To be fair, a strong season helps. In the third quarter of twenty twenty-six, AMC made a one hundred nine point three million dollar profit. Full breakdown on Pricewhy.

## Short 5 — The popcorn cost nobody publishes
- **Publish:** Wed 9 Dec
- **Hook text:** The popcorn cost nobody publishes
- **Narration (≈ 80 words):** How much does a tub of movie popcorn cost the theater? Honest answer: nobody publishes it. Not AMC, not Cinemark. But their annual reports do show the cost of everything sold at the counter, combined. In twenty twenty-five, AMC paid about eighteen cents in product cost for every dollar of food and drink it sold in the US. Cinemark paid about nineteen. That's before wages. And plain popcorn very likely costs even less than that average. Full breakdown on Pricewhy.
