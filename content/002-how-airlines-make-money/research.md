# Research: How airlines really make money
Researched: 2026-11-20

**Scope and method.** The latest full fiscal year for Delta, United and American is **FY2025** (calendar year ended Dec 31, 2025). Their FY2025 10-Ks were filed in February 2026 (Delta Feb 10, United Feb 12, American Feb 18). Delta and American numbers below were read in the 10-K text or the company's own earnings release. 2026 numbers are guidance or partial-year results (latest: Delta's Q3 2026 call on Oct 9, 2026). The 2020-2021 loyalty financings were checked against the companies' SEC filings or press releases where reachable.

**Confidence labels:**
- **proven:** read in a primary document (10-K, 8-K, earnings release, investor deck, earnings-call transcript published by the company, IATA release, government press release).
- **probable:** a reputable outlet (Reuters, Fortune, CNN, Skift, Aviation Week, Canadian Press) reporting directly, or a primary source seen only through a reliable secondary quote.
- **uncertain:** single secondary source, unclear method, a model or estimate, or a self-interested source.

Any figure marked **(calc)** is my own arithmetic from the cited numbers. The arithmetic is shown in the last sections.

> **Thesis test, short version.**
> 1. "Big US airlines earn a huge share of their profit from selling miles to banks." **Directionally supported, but the word "profit" needs care.** Airlines disclose the *cash* banks pay them, not the *profit* on it. That cash is larger than all their pre-tax profit:
>    - Delta 2025: $8.2B from Amex vs $5.0B adjusted pre-tax.
>    - American 2025: $6.2B from card and other partners vs $190M pre-tax.
>
>    The only primary "share of profit" figure found is United's own: in 2019, MileagePlus produced ~26% of United's adjusted EBITDAR from ~12% of revenue.
> 2. "Loyalty programs have been valued at more than the airlines themselves." **Supported for 2020**, with caveats:
>    - United valued MileagePlus at ~$21.9B. United's market value was ~$10B (that figure is secondhand).
>    - A third-party appraisal put AAdvantage at $19.5-31.5B, while American's market cap was under $10B.
>
>    The caveats: these are appraisals or multiples, not market prices. And market cap is equity *after* debt, so it is not a like-for-like comparison.

## Loyalty program mechanics

**1. Banks buy miles; airlines sell them.**
- Delta's 10-K says it plainly: "We sell miles to American Express which are then provided to their customers under the co-brand credit card program and the Membership Rewards program." Payments are "typically due to us monthly based on the volume of miles sold." Initial contract terms run "from one to thirteen years." (Delta FY2025 10-K, Note on revenue recognition) **proven**
- United: "United has a contract (the 'Co-Brand Agreement') to sell MileagePlus miles to its co-branded credit card partner Chase." (United FY2025 10-K, Critical Accounting Policies) **proven**
- The bank gets interchange ("swipe") fees, annual fees and interest. Amex says the Delta portfolio "generates fee revenue and interest income from Card Members and discount revenue from Delta and other merchants." (Amex 2025 Annual Report) **proven**

**2. What the bank actually buys is a bundle, not just miles.** Under Delta's accounting, each Amex payment is split by "relative selling prices" across:
- award travel;
- baggage-fee waivers;
- lounge access;
- priority boarding;
- "the use of our brand."

Delta values the brand piece "using estimated royalties generated from the use of our brand." (Delta 10-K) **proven** The same structure appears at United, where the components are miles, marketing, advertising, lounge passes, bag fees and seat upgrades (United 10-K). **proven**

**3. Revenue recognition (why "cash" is not equal to "revenue this year").**
- **The travel piece is deferred.** It goes to "loyalty program deferred revenue," a liability, and becomes passenger revenue ("Loyalty travel awards") only when the member flies the award seat. Delta's balance: $9.3B at end-2025.
- **The brand and marketing piece is recognized right away**, as "Loyalty program" other revenue: $3,362M at Delta in 2025.
- **Bag waivers and lounge access** are recognized when used. (Delta 10-K) **proven**
- A Delta spokesperson told Reuters that part of the cash is recognized immediately and some is deferred until miles are redeemed. (Reuters, Mar 13, 2026) **probable**
- American's 10-K uses the same model. Its loyalty liability was $10.56B at end-2025 (2025 deferrals $4.45B; recognized $3.94B). **proven**

**4. Breakage (miles never redeemed).**
- Airlines estimate breakage statistically and do not book it as a windfall on day one. Delta: "We recognize mileage breakage proportionally during the period in which the remaining miles are actually redeemed." **proven**
- Sensitivity: "A hypothetical 10% change in the number of outstanding miles estimated to be redeemed would result in an impact of less than 1% of total operating revenue." (Delta FY2025 10-K) **proven**
- **No current breakage percentage is disclosed** by Delta, United or American in the passages reviewed. See "Do NOT claim."

**5. Reported price per mile. Only examples and estimates exist.**
- **United's June 2020 investor deck** worked an example: a partner buying miles at **$0.02 per mile**, with MileagePlus paying United a redemption rate fixed at **$0.01 per mile** for award seats. That implies a ~50% gross margin on third-party miles in the example. The deck also says the price at which United buys miles from MileagePlus carries a guaranteed minimum EBITDA margin of 20% for MileagePlus. (SEC Ex. 99.1, June 15, 2020) **proven that the deck said this; uncertain that 2 cents is the real Chase price.** Cranky Flier notes large buyers like Chase likely pay less.
- **AAdvantage 2021 deck:** "Purchase price per mile negotiated at the partner level." No figure is given. **proven**
- **Blogger estimate (Gary Leff, Sept 18, 2026):** $8.2B ÷ (13% × $1,669.8B Amex billed business) ≈ **3.8 cents per dollar charged** on Delta cards. At an assumed ~1.5 miles per dollar, that is ~2.5 cents per mile. https://viewfromthewing.com/amex-pays-delta-3-8-cents-per-dollar-charged-why-banks-pay-more-for-miles-than-you-do/ **uncertain** (the miles-per-dollar input is assumed)
- **Durbin (Senate floor, May 9, 2024)** on consumer purchases: buying points can cost "three cents to purchase a point worth roughly one cent." **proven as his statement**
- **TD Cowen's Tom Fitzgerald** (via CNN, Sept 8, 2024) put the margin on the flight side of miles sales at ~50%. https://finance.yahoo.com/news/frequent-flyer-programs-most-profitable-160019355.html **probable**

**6. What the airline gets vs what a reward seat costs it.**
- **AAdvantage 2019 pro forma** is the clearest primary view (**proven**):
  - the program took in **$5.91B** in cash from selling miles;
  - it paid **$2.64B** for redemptions (award seats bought from the airline, partner awards, etc.) and $0.13B in opex;
  - net operating cash flow was **$3.15B (53.2% of cash received)**.

  Note: this is a carve-out built for the financing. Its internal seat prices are set by contract.
- **Reward seats are a minority of flying.** Award travel was 12% of revenue miles at Delta, ~10.3% of RPMs at United and ~9% of RPMs at American in 2025. (10-Ks) **proven**
- **Average award revenue per Delta award ticket ≈ $121** (calc: $4,237M loyalty travel awards ÷ ~35M award tickets). **uncertain**, because the 35M may include tickets on partner airlines and the revenue line is Delta-only. Use only as "roughly."
- **No airline discloses its marginal cost of flying an award passenger.** The "it's an empty seat anyway" argument is an inference, not a disclosed number.

**7. Not every dollar of bank cash is profit. American's own projection proves it.**
- American told investors that partner cash rising toward ~$10B/yr (from ~$5.6B TTM in 2024) would add ~**$1.5B** of annual pre-tax income.
- Calc: $1.5B ÷ ($10B − $5.6B) ≈ **34 cents of pre-tax profit per extra $1 of partner cash**. Measured against the 2024 full-year $6.1B instead, it is ≈ 38 cents. **probable** (8-K seen via quote)
- This is the best available disclosure of the marginal profitability of co-brand cash.

**8. Sizes of the card economy.**
- Delta cobrand ≈ 13% of all Amex billed business worldwide and 21% of Amex card loans (Amex AR 2025). **proven**
- Spend on Delta cards ≈ $217B in 2025 (calc). That is ~3.7× Delta's own adjusted revenue ($58.3B). **probable**
- A4A: ~63% of miles earned in 2022 came from airline credit cards. **probable**

## Nuance and counterpoints

- **Cash is not profit.**
  - Remuneration is gross cash. Part of it is deferred and later "spent" on award seats, bag waivers and lounge access, which have real costs.
  - American's own 8-K projection implies ~34-38 cents of pre-tax profit per incremental $1 of partner cash, not $1.
  - So "Amex cash > Delta's profit" does **not** mean "Delta loses money flying." It means the loyalty contract is very large relative to profit.
- **The programs can't exist without the airline.**
  - Bank of America analyst Andrew Didora: "It's very difficult to separate out the value of the programs and the value of the rest of the airline... They can't exist without each other." (CNN, Sept 2024) **probable**
  - Miles are only worth something because there is a network to fly on and seats to redeem.
- **The Aeroplan precedent.** As a standalone company under Aimia, Aeroplan's value eroded. Air Canada bought it back in 2019 for ~C$450M. The Motley Fool (June 2020) used this to argue AAdvantage's $19.5-31.5B appraisal was "wildly overvaluing" it. **probable**
- **Market cap vs enterprise value.**
  - Loyalty valuations are enterprise-style values. Airline market caps are equity after billions of debt (American: $30.7B net debt).
  - Skift made exactly this caveat about Aeroplan.
  - Against EV, the "worth 4×" headlines shrink. For American 2026, AAdvantage is ~71% of EV (calc).
- **Model valuations are soft.**
  - On Point's rankings rest on undisclosed per-program data and assume a minority-stake sale.
  - Gary Leff (View from the Wing, Mar 31, 2026) says the presentation is "more precise than the method supports."
  - United's $21.9B was a self-chosen 12× EBITDA multiple: an assumption, not a market price.
- **The airlines themselves resist monetizing.** Delta's Bastian (Oct 2026): "I'm really reticent to put a third party, a financial investor between us and our best customers."
- **Cyclicality.** Payments analyst Brian Riley (Reuters, Mar 2026): in a downturn, banks tighten lending and cut co-brand marketing, hitting airline earnings within two to three quarters. **probable**
- **Regulatory and devaluation risk.**
  - IdeaWorks says reward value to members has roughly halved since 2019.
  - Interchange caps in Europe and Australia reduced rewards and raised annual fees (per Reuters' summary of research).
  - The CCCA and a 10% rate cap would hit the bank side of the equation.
- **Who pays?** The National Retail Federation (Reuters, Mar 2026) says premium cards carry the highest interchange rates, so non-users help subsidize card users. This is a contested view, so attribute it.
- **The airline counter-narrative.** Alaska's loyalty chief says non-cardholders "continue to earn meaningful value through flying." Airlines reject the claim that cards are replacing flying. (Reuters, Mar 2026) **probable**
- **The "flying loses money" meme is a model, not a fact.** Estimates like "Delta's physical airline is worth negative $6B" (Byrne Hobart via The Hustle) or "airlines are worthless" (Wendover Productions) are subtraction exercises on contested valuations. Do not present them as findings.

## Breakdown for chart (with arithmetic)

**Can we honestly say "Of every $1 of Delta's profit in year X, Y cents came from Amex"? No.** Three reasons:
1. Delta discloses Amex *remuneration* (cash), not the *profit* on it.
2. Part of that cash is deferred and later consumed by award-seat, lounge and bag-waiver costs that Delta does not break out.
3. The cash is larger than total profit, so any "cents per $1 of profit" share would exceed 100 cents. That is mathematically meaningless as a slice of a pie.

The honest charts below *are* derivable from sources.

**Chart A: "For every $1 of Delta pre-tax profit, Amex paid Delta..."** (cash ÷ profit; not a share of profit)

| Year | Amex remuneration | Delta pre-tax (adjusted) | Ratio (calc) | Ratio on GAAP pre-tax (calc) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | $7.4B | $5.201B | 7.4 ÷ 5.201 = **$1.42** | 7.4 ÷ 4.658 = $1.59 |
| 2025 | $8.2B | $4.981B | 8.2 ÷ 4.981 = **$1.65** | 8.2 ÷ 6.185 = $1.33 |
| 2026E (guidance) | >$9B | ~$4.5B | >9 ÷ 4.5 = **>$2.00** | n/a |
| Q2 2026 (single quarter) | $2.4B | $1.4B | 2.4 ÷ 1.4 = $1.71 | 2.4 ÷ 2.0 = $1.20 |

Caveats:
- Use **adjusted** pre-tax as the main bar. 2025 GAAP pre-tax is inflated by a $1.21B investment gain.
- On screen, label it "cash received from Amex" vs "pre-tax profit," never "profit from Amex."
- 2026 is guidance as of Oct 9, 2026; the actual FY2026 figures arrive in Jan 2027.

**Chart B: "Of every $1 Delta took in during 2025, ~14 cents was a check from Amex."**
- Calc: $8.2B ÷ $58.287B adjusted operating revenue = **14.1 cents**. On GAAP revenue ($63.364B): 12.9 cents.
- Reuters independently states "about 14% of adjusted operating revenue."
- Caveat: not all of the $8.2B is recognized as revenue in 2025 (part is deferred), so this is a cash-to-revenue ratio.

**Chart C: the only true "share of profit" from a primary source is United, 2019.**
- From United's own June 2020 deck: MileagePlus = **~12 cents of every $1 of United revenue**, but **~26 cents of every $1 of United's adjusted EBITDAR**.
- Caveats:
  - EBITDAR (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization and aircraft rent) is not net profit.
  - The split depends on internal transfer prices United set: MileagePlus buys award seats from United at a contract rate, with a guaranteed minimum 20% EBITDA margin on United-related miles.
  - The data is pre-pandemic (2019).

**Chart D: American, 2025, same scale.**
- Bars: **$6,200M** partner cash vs **$1,467M** operating income vs **$190M** pre-tax vs **$111M** net income.
- Ratios (calc): 6.2 ÷ 1.467 ≈ **4.2×** operating income (Reuters: "roughly four times its adjusted operating income"); 6.2 ÷ 0.190 ≈ **33×** pre-tax.
- Caveat: same as Chart A. This is cash vs profit, not profit attribution.

**Chart E: "Not every dollar is profit." American's own projection.**
- (~$10B − ~$5.6B) ≈ $4.4B more partner cash → ~$1.5B more pre-tax.
- Calc: ≈ **34 cents of profit per extra $1**; ≈ 38 cents if measured from 2024's $6.1B.
- A good honesty beat right after Chart D.

**Chart F: 2019 loyalty revenue vs net income** (Ladd & Luttmann, Table 2, from 10-Ks)
- Delta $4,862M vs $4,767M
- United $4,350M vs $3,009M
- American $5,540M vs $1,972M
- Southwest $3,787M vs $2,300M
- Alaska $1,169M vs $769M

Caveat: the American net income in that table ($1,972M) differs from AAG's reported $1,686M. The table likely uses the American Airlines, Inc. subsidiary. If shown, cite the paper and note it.

## Do NOT claim (myths or numbers you could not verify)

- **"X cents of every dollar of Delta's profit comes from Amex."** Not derivable; see the chart section. Also avoid "Delta makes more profit from Amex than from flying." Delta does not disclose profit by source.
- **"Airlines lose money on flying" / "the airline part is worth negative $X."** This is model-dependent (Byrne Hobart, Wendover Productions, the Vanderbilt student note) and not in any filing.
- **"Delta's SkyMiles was appraised at $26 billion in 2020"** as a fact. Only secondhand (FT analysis via Simple Flying/The Hustle; a Vanderbilt note citing a YouTube video). If used, say "a Financial Times analysis estimated about $26B."
- **"Banks pay 2 cents per mile"** as a fact. That was a worked example in United's 2020 deck; real contract prices are confidential. Leff's ~2.5 cents is an estimate with an assumed earn rate.
- **Any specific breakage percentage** (e.g., "20-30% of miles are never used"). Not disclosed in the current Delta, United or American 10-K passages reviewed. An old ~18% United assumption predates United's 2019 removal of mile expiration.
- **"Delta's card is 10% of Amex billings"** (Bastian, via Fortune Apr 2026). It conflicts with Amex's 10-K figure of ~13%; use the 10-K.
- **"Delta card spending is ~1% of US GDP."** This is a CEO claim (Fortune, Apr 2026), not independently verified. If used, attribute it to Bastian.
- **"Amex is ~10% of Delta's revenue" (Fortune).** Use the Reuters/filings-based ~14% of adjusted revenue (12.9% of GAAP) instead.
- **"SkyMiles is worth $75 billion."** That was an analyst's offhand hypothetical on an earnings call, not a valuation.
- **"DOT found/fined airlines over loyalty devaluations."** No findings were published. The latest report (Reuters, Mar 2026) says responses are under review.
- **"The Credit Card Competition Act passed / got a 2026 Senate vote."** No floor vote was found through Oct 2026.
- **"57% of miles came from credit cards" or "63% of miles came from credit cards" as neutral facts.** Both are industry claims: the 57% (2023) comes from the airlines' June 2025 lobbying letter (via Reuters), and the 63% (2022) from A4A. If used, attribute them: "according to the airlines' own trade group..."
- **"Amex prepaid Delta $1B in April 2020."** Not verified. A $1B advance purchase of SkyMiles is reported for **2008** (Fortune, Apr 2026; Skift). The verified April 2020 $1B Amex prepurchase was of **Hilton** points.
- **CFPB Circular 2024-07 is "in force" or "withdrawn."** Status as of Nov 2026 is unverified.
- **The 2026 full-year Delta Amex figure as final.** ">$9B" is guidance; the actual figure comes with FY2026 results (expected Jan 2027).
- **United's dollar amount from Chase.** United does not disclose a Chase remuneration dollar figure comparable to Delta's. Only growth rates (+12% in 2025) and the 2019 deck numbers are available.
- **Quoting Durbin as "United is a credit card company that owns some planes."** That wording was reported secondhand. The verified line from his office is "Modern day airlines have become credit card companies that also happen to own airplanes."
- **2020 airline market caps as precise figures.** The ~$10B United and ~$6B American numbers come from an FT analysis seen secondhand, and the date of measurement varies. Say "roughly."
